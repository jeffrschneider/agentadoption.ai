An open framework

Companies adopt agents one department at a time, in four levels.

The Agent Adoption Framework describes how a department moves from employees using personal assistants on their own to a mesh of agents that runs its work and improves itself. Use it to see where each department stands today and what it takes to reach the next level.

The four levels

Each level adds one thing a department could not do before.

A department climbs one level at a time, because each level depends on the one below it. The checklist under each level says what must be true before the department is there.

0
Level 0

Bespoke

Employees use personal assistants on their own.

  • Each person has an assistant, a few saved prompts and skills, and their own habits.
  • Nothing is shared, nothing lasts past the task, and every person spends their own tokens.
  • Nobody can say which agents the department relies on.

Where most companies start

Level 0 is the starting point. The good news is that the skills people already reuse are the raw material for level 1.

1
Level 1

Activated

The department's agents live in one mesh, with owners and rules.

  • The skills that keep working become departmental agents.
  • Agents come from five sources: skills, generated drafts, written processes, SaaS and custom builds.
  • Employees and other agents can find each agent and use it.

To reach level 1

  1. Every agent the department relies on has a named owner.
  2. Each agent has its own access and budget, separate from any person's.
  3. Each agent publishes a card that says what it does and who may use it.
2
Level 2

Orchestrated

Workflows connect employees and agents, and run on their own.

  • A process manager starts each workflow when an event happens or on a schedule.
  • Each workflow names its agents and what each one takes in and passes on.
  • People approve the decisions that are theirs before agents act.

To reach level 2

  1. Work starts on events and schedules without anyone prompting an agent.
  2. Every hand-off between agents is written down, with its inputs and outputs.
  3. Approvals are part of the workflow and assigned to named people.
3
Level 3

Self-improving

The mesh learns from every run and improves its workflows and agents.

  • Every run is recorded against the department's goals.
  • The mesh proposes changes, such as sending follow-ups sooner or moving budget between agents.
  • Owners approve each change, and the next run starts from the better version.

To reach level 3

  1. Every run is recorded with what each agent did, what it cost and what came of it.
  2. Changes are proposed from that record and measured against the goals.
  3. An owner approves each change before it takes effect.

Five sources of agents

A department's first agents come from five places, and three of them are enough to reach level 1.

Each source comes with one question. Ask it inside the department, and the answers become the mesh's first list of agents.

Three jobs

Three jobs move a department up the levels, and each one unlocks the next level.

A person, a team or an agent can do each job. What matters is that someone does it, for every department, all the time.

Principles

Five principles keep adoption moving across the whole company.

Adopt by department.

The department is the unit of adoption: it has goals, a budget and people who are accountable for both.

Start with what already works.

Most departments already have agents in the skills their people reuse and the processes they have written down.

Keep people on the decisions that are theirs.

At every level, approvals belong to named owners, and agents act inside the limits those owners set.

Climb one level at a time.

Workflows need agents with owners, and improvement needs workflows that record their runs.

Map every department.

Departments climb at different speeds. Draw the company as a building, one column per department, and light each floor it has reached.
